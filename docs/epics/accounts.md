# Epic 2: Accounts and sign-in

## Status

⏳ In progress: 2.1, 2.2 and 2.7 are done.

## Overview

Sign-in identical to Fjord Web's: local accounts, a Sovereign core, or both, on better-auth. Everything Fjord tested about it is tested here. Also
Settings, what a person leaves by (which now hands shared work on to the people still using it), and what makes the app hostable by an instance.

## Related

[`DESIGN.md`](../../DESIGN.md) §6, §19, §22, §24 · Fjord's `clients/web/DESIGN.md` §6, §17 and `docs/epics/web-1a.md` 1a.2–1a.4

## Tasks

#### ✅ 2.1 — Local sign-in

**Goal:** someone can make the first account, sign in and sign out.

**Design:** §6. **Depends on:** 1.5, 1.8.

**Technical:**

- `better-auth` and `@better-auth/drizzle-adapter`, pinned to one `~1.7` version, in the auth store, with its own migrations in `migrations/auth/` and tables in `src/db/schema/*/auth.ts`.
- `src/auth/config.ts`: `TASKS_AUTH_LOCAL`, `TASKS_AUTH_SECRET` (32 characters or more), `TASKS_URL`, `TASKS_AUTH_SIGNUP` (`closed` by default). A wrong setting stops the server.
- `src/auth/server.ts`; `app/api/auth/[...all]/route.ts`. `src/auth/session.ts`: `requireAccount()`, returning the account id, which everything after this uses.
- Sign-up policy in the user-creation hook, so that it holds for every path: with `closed`, the first account and then refused, with code `SIGNUP_CLOSED`.
- Off means off: `/sign-in`, `/sign-up` and `/api/auth` answer 404. Screens from the design system, `force-dynamic`, and no message says whether an address has an account.
- `UserMenu` in `AppShell`, with Sign out; the rest of sign-out's work is 13.4.
- `src/auth/test-support.ts`: tests go through `auth.handler` with real cookies and `Origin`, not a mock.

**Accepts when:** sign up, sign in and sign out work in a browser and over real requests on all three databases; a second sign-up is refused by the server on `closed`; the origin check and the lockout are each seen to fail with them switched off.

**Done as:** adapted from Fjord Web's sign-in, with Tasks' names, `better-auth` and its Drizzle adapter pinned to `~1.7.16`. `src/auth/config.ts` is read by `loadConfig` with the other settings, so the server stops on a wrong one, naming it and never its value: `TASKS_AUTH_LOCAL` (1 or 0; anything else is refused, not guessed), `TASKS_AUTH_SECRET` or `_FILE` (32 characters or more), `TASKS_URL` (required once local sign-in is on), `TASKS_AUTH_SIGNUP` (`closed` by default) and `TASKS_AUTH_PASSWORD_MIN_LENGTH` (10, from 8 to 128; not in the epic, taken from Fjord). The auth store has its first migration, `auth_tables`, for `user`, `session`, `account`, `verification` and `rate_limit`, with a Drizzle config and a folder per dialect under `migrations/auth/`; `pnpm db:generate` writes all four and CI's check covers them, the parity test covers them, and `runMigrations` no longer has an `optional` mode now that the auth store has migrations to apply. `createAuth` takes settings and a database and nothing global; the sign-up policy is in the user-creation hook, so it holds for every path, and refuses with `403` and code `SIGNUP_CLOSED`. Sessions are cookies prefixed `tasks`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Secure` over https; the origin check is on explicitly (better-auth turns it off under `NODE_ENV=test`); rate limits are counted in the database. `requireAccount()` returns the better-auth user id and sends a visitor to `/sign-in`. Off means off: `/api/auth` answers 404 from its handler, and `/sign-in` and `/sign-up` from the proxy, which rewrites them to the page that is not found (a page cannot do it itself: the root loading state has already sent a 200 by the time `notFound()` runs). Screens use the design system's auth components through `src/ui`; no message says whether an address has an account (a wrong password and an unknown address were seen to answer the same, in the API and in a browser). `AccountMenu` in `AppShell`'s `account` slot has Sign out, which ends the session; clearing the local copy of a person's tasks is 13.4's. Tests: in `src/auth`, 10 over real requests through `auth.handler` on a fresh auth store, with real cookies and an `Origin` (a first account; a hash and not the password; the cookie's flags; later sign-ups refused with the code and nothing created; sign-in and sign-out, and a replayed cookie after sign-out; the rate limit; a hostile and a missing `Origin`; a short password; open sign-up with each account separate and a taken address refused); 5 of the settings; 2 of the route when off and when on; one of the proxy; and 4 in Chromium and WebKit. The whole suite ran, passing, on sqld, a file and PostgreSQL 16, and the browser tests on all three. Seen to fail: the origin check switched off, the rate limit switched off, and the sign-up policy removed, each failing exactly its own test and no other. Not as the epic said: the "lockout" is the per-address rate limit (three tries in ten seconds, then 429), since account lockout in better-auth belongs to two-factor, which is 2.4; and the auth tables keep their foreign keys with `ON DELETE CASCADE` as Fjord's do, since the rule against them is for the app store (DESIGN.md §22.1) and better-auth relies on them to remove a person's sessions and accounts. Two-factor and passkeys add their tables and `two_factor_enabled` with 2.4. Not done: `src/access/routes.ts` and its test of who may call a route, which is 3.1 (`/api/auth` is public when sign-in is on and absent when it is off, and the route test says so); the `?next=` return address, which is not needed until there is a page to return to; the manifest's sign-in paths, which 2.10 replaces. It has not run on GitHub: nothing is pushed.

#### ✅ 2.2 — Landing page and the address `/`

**Goal:** `/` says what Tasks is to a visitor, and is the first list to someone signed in, at the same address.

**Design:** §17.11. **Depends on:** 2.1.

**Technical:** a static page from typed content, in `src/landing/`. `proxy.ts` rewrites `/` to `/home` when a session cookie is present, looking only at the cookie. `/home` checks the session, and until 3.3 is a placeholder; then it starts the app's shell (13.4), which opens the first list.

**Accepts when:** a visitor gets static HTML in light and dark at a phone's width; a signed-in person sees their list at `/`; a forged cookie gets the sign-in page, not data.

**Done as:** `src/landing/content.ts` is the page's words, typed, and a test refuses any that promise what is not built (encryption, reminders, sharing, backup, repeat, push, subtasks); `Landing` is a server component with no client code in it, so it is the same HTML before any script and with none (seen in a browser with JavaScript off): a heading, a lead, _Sign in_ where sign-in is set up at all, _Create an account_ only where the server would take one, and three short statements of what is so today. `app/page.tsx` is that page inside the shell. **`proxy.ts` rewrites `/` to `/home` when a session cookie is present**, looking only at the cookie (`src/auth/cookie.ts`, which the proxy can import because it has no Node in it, and which a test holds to the name better-auth sets, with and without the `Secure` prefix) and not for an empty value, another name or another page; the address stays `/`. `/home` checks the session itself and shows the first list, in the sidebar's order, from the working copy (`HomeScreen`), so **a forged cookie gets the sign-in page and no data** (seen in a browser), and so does a cookie the server has ended. Signing in, signing out, and the link on a missing list now go to `/`. The placeholder text "Tasks is being built" is gone, and the browser tests that used it as the sign that the app was there look for the lists instead. The proxy is tested for each case above; 4 browser tests in Chromium and WebKit (a visitor with no script, an account offered only where it can be made, the first list at `/` and the front page again after signing out, a forged cookie), and the dark and phone-width tests of the shell cover the page as before. Looked at in a screenshot. One thing the rewrite changed: closing a task's detail replaces the address with the _path of the page_, which is `/home` when the page was opened there by address and `/` when it was reached at the front, so a link to `/home?task=` stays at `/home`.

#### ✅ 2.3 — Email: verification and reset

**Goal:** with SMTP set, addresses are verified and a forgotten password can be reset. Without it, neither is offered.

**Design:** §6. **Depends on:** 2.1, 15.1.

**Technical:** `nodemailer` behind an interface in `src/mail/`, with a test mailer that a test can read, and templates. `TUSK_SMTP_HOST`, `_PORT`,
`_USER`, `_PASSWORD` (`_FILE`), `_FROM`; a host with no sender stops the server. With it, verification before first sign-in (the link signs in) and a
single-use reset that ends every session. Without it, no screen offers a reset, and the endpoint is off.

**Accepts when:** each flow runs end to end against the test mailer; a reset link used twice is refused; a reset ends other sessions; without SMTP the
reset endpoint answers as absent.

**Done as:** **`src/mail/`**: `Mailer` is one call, `send({ to, subject, text })`; `smtpMailer` is `nodemailer` (10.3.1, MIT-0, exact-pinned) with timeouts so that a mail server that cannot be reached does not hold a sign-up open; `MemoryMailer` keeps what it is given for a test, can be made to fail, and finds the last link to an address; `templates.ts` makes the two messages, plain text with one link each. **`TUSK_SMTP_HOST`, `_PORT` (587; 465 is SMTPS), `_USER` and `_PASSWORD` (or `_PASSWORD_FILE`; both or neither) and `_FROM`** are read by `resolveMail` into `Config.mail`: a host with no sender, a port that is not one, a host written as an address with a scheme, a sender that is not an address (or has a line break in it, which would add a header), and a user without a password stop the server at start-up, naming the setting and never a value. **With a mailer** (`createAuth({ mailer })`): a new account is made and **not signed in**, and one link is sent; **a sign-in before the address is confirmed is refused (`EMAIL_NOT_VERIFIED`) and a new link is sent; the link signs the person in**; a forgotten password is asked for by address, **answered the same whether or not there is an account**, and the message goes only to one that has (sent without being waited for, so that how long a reply takes does not say either); the reset link is **good once and for an hour, a password that is too short does not spend it, using it twice is refused, and using it ends every other session of the account.** **Without mail none of it exists:** a new account is signed in at once as before, `/forgot-password` and `/reset-password` are 404, no screen offers a reset, and the routes under `/api/auth` that would send or use a link (`request-password-reset`, `reset-password`, `send-verification-email`, `verify-email`) answer **404 as routes that are not there** (`src/auth/mail-routes.ts`, applied in the route and named in a test). The sign-in page has "Forgot your password?" only with mail; the sign-up page says to check the email. Tests: the settings (none without a host, the defaults, every refusal, the password from a file, through the whole configuration); the messages (one link, what it says, no secret); the memory mailer; **the SMTP mailer against a real SMTP conversation** (a small server in `src/mail/testing/` that nodemailer speaks to: the envelope, the headers, the link in the body once the quoted-printable encoding is undone) and failing without hanging when nothing is listening; and **each flow end to end against the sign-in library with a readable mailer, on whichever database the leg runs**: the account not signed in, one link, the refusal before confirmation, the link signing in, a link that is not good, a new link on an unconfirmed sign-in, the same answer for an address with no account, a reset once and not twice, the old password dead, other sessions ended, a token that is not one, a short password not spending the link, and a mail server that fails changing no answer. **Each rule was seen to fail with it broken:** the verification requirement, the revoking of sessions, the sign-in by the link and the 404 for the reset route. **Not run:** a browser test of the screens (they are thin forms over these calls; it needs a mail server in the browser test's instance, which the SMTP sink could be); the same tests on sqld and PostgreSQL (Docker was not running then; the unit suite has since passed on both, task 2.9); a real mail server.

#### ✅ 2.4 — Two-factor and passkeys

**Goal:** an authenticator app with backup codes, and passkeys.

**Design:** §6, §19. **Depends on:** 2.1, 2.7.

**Technical:** better-auth's `twoFactor` plugin and `@better-auth/passkey`. `qrcode.react` for the enrolment code. Ten single-use backup codes, turned
on only once a code has been shown to work, each change asking for the password. Passkeys added, named, removed, and able to sign in with no password;
the relying party comes from `TUSK_URL`. In Settings > Security.

**Accepts when:** done in a real browser with Playwright's virtual authenticator, on all three databases: enrol, sign in with each, recover with a backup
code, a spent code refused.

**Done as:** better-auth's `twoFactor` plugin and `@better-auth/passkey` 1.7.16 (exact-pinned, the version of the installed better-auth; its dependency `@simplewebauthn/server` comes with it), and `qrcode.react` 4.2.0 for the code to scan. The auth store gained `user.two_factor_enabled`, `two_factor` and `passkey` (migration `auth/*/0001_auth_two_factor_passkey`, both dialects; the plugins' own shapes). **The authenticator app:** asking to begin takes the password and gives the address of a secret (as a QR code and, for a person who cannot scan, as text to type) and ten backup codes; **it is not on until a code from the app has been shown to work**, so a mistaken scan cannot lock a person out; sign-in then asks for a code after the password (there is no session until it is given), a backup code stands in for the app and **works once**, and **turning it off and making new backup codes (which end the old ones) ask for the password again.** The library locks an account's second step after ten failed tries for fifteen minutes. **Passkeys** are added from the device (the browser asks), named, renamed and removed in Settings > Security; one **signs in with no password** from "Use a passkey instead" on the sign-in page, shown only where the browser has passkeys; the relying party is the address in `TUSK_URL`, so a passkey works there and nowhere else. Tests: the authenticator over real requests on whichever database the leg runs, with the code worked out from the secret by RFC 6238 (not on until a code worked, wrong codes, the second step needed for a session, a backup code once, new codes ending old ones and needing the password, off needing the password, one account's app not another's, the second step refused to someone who has not done the first); and **a browser test on a third instance** (its own SQLite file and its own first account, because these change how the account signs in): in Chromium and WebKit the authenticator is set up (a wrong password and a wrong code refused), the backup codes are shown, sign-in asks for a code, a backup code signs in once and not twice, the app's code signs in, and turning it off asks for the password; **in Chromium, with its virtual authenticator over the DevTools protocol, a passkey is added, named, renamed, signs in with no password, and is removed, after which it signs in nothing.** **Each rule was seen to fail with it broken:** the turning on only after a code (`skipVerificationOnEnable`). `deletePerson` (2.8) removes the second factors and passkeys by name before the account; the store would cascade them too (a test cannot tell the two apart), and `people.test.ts` was the thing that said these tables had to be decided. **Left out, and said so:** the epic said "on all three databases"; the authenticator tests run on the leg's database and the passkey table is made for both dialects, but sqld and PostgreSQL have not been run here (Docker was not running then; the unit suite has since passed on both, task 2.9). A failed backup code ends that sign-in attempt in the library, so the password is given again, which the browser test does.

#### ✅ 2.5 — Sovereign sign-in

**Goal:** sign-in through a core, with a server that also works when that core is down.

**Design:** §6. **Depends on:** 2.1, 15.1.

**Technical:**

- `TUSK_CORE_URL`, `TUSK_CORE_CLIENT_ID`, `TUSK_CORE_CLIENT_SECRET`: all or none, a partial set stops the server.
- better-auth's `genericOAuth` with discovery: PKCE S256, basic auth, a nonce, `requireIdTokenVerification`. Redirect `/api/auth/callback/sovereign`;
  scopes `openid profile email` in one constant the manifest test shares. `sub` is the account id; `email` and `name` are copied once.
  `encryptOAuthTokens`, `disableProviderLogout`.
- Refused with a code and a message, never bare JSON: `account_not_linked`, `SIGNUP_CLOSED`.
- `src/auth/testing/fake-sovereign.ts`: a strict OIDC provider (exact redirect, PKCE, secret, single-use codes, EdDSA).
  `scripts/record-sovereign-discovery.mjs`, and a test tying the fake to a recording of a real document.

**Accepts when:** sign-in works against the fake, in tests and in a browser; a token with a wrong signature, audience or nonce is refused; a core
unreachable at start-up does not stop the server; a local account's email coming through Sovereign is refused.

**Done as:** adapted from Fjord Web's sign-in. **Settings** (`src/auth/config.ts`): a core is set with `TUSK_CORE_URL`, `TUSK_CORE_CLIENT_ID` and `TUSK_CORE_CLIENT_SECRET[_FILE]`, all or none (a partial set stops the server, naming what is missing and never a value); the address must be https (http for localhost), with no user, query or fragment, and is kept as the issuer without a trailing slash (a path is allowed, as the recording of a real core has one). **Sovereign alone is a way in:** `AuthSettings` has `local` and `sovereign`, so with a core and no `TUSK_AUTH_LOCAL` the server needs `TUSK_URL` and `TUSK_AUTH_SECRET` as before, `/sign-in` exists and shows only "Sign in with Sovereign", and `/sign-up`, `/forgot-password` and `/reset-password` answer 404 from the proxy (`src/config/edge.ts` now also says whether a core is set); email and password, the two-factor and passkey plugins and mail are not mounted at all without `local`. **`createAuth`** adds better-auth's `genericOAuth` with discovery, PKCE S256, the secret by basic auth, `requireIdTokenVerification` (a discovery document with no keys is refused, not trusted), the scopes in one constant (`SOVEREIGN_SCOPES`, which the manifest test of 2.10 will hold the manifest to), `disableProviderLogout` (signing out here does not sign out of core) and `encryptOAuthTokens`; the account is the better-auth user made from the `sub` (the account id of the Sovereign row), with `email` and `name` copied when it is made and a name taken from the address when core sends none. **Linking is configured for 2.6 and no further:** implicit linking is off and different addresses are not allowed, so a Sovereign login whose address is a local account's is refused (`account_not_linked`), and the last way in is never unlinked; the screens for linking are 2.6's. The sign-up policy is still the user-creation hook's, so a second person through Sovereign on a closed instance is refused with `SIGNUP_CLOSED` and sent back to `/sign-in?error=SIGNUP_CLOSED`. **The page** shows what went wrong on the way back (`oauthErrorMessage`: a declined consent, a closed sign-up, an address that is a local account's, an expired start), each in a sentence of ours that says nothing more about an account than the person knows. **The fake** (`src/auth/testing/fake-sovereign.ts`, `jose` as an exact-pinned dev dependency): discovery, keys, authorization (who is at the keyboard is set by the test), token and userinfo; the redirect is matched exactly, PKCE is checked, the client signs in with its secret, a code works once, the ID token is EdDSA, and a test can sign with a key it does not publish or leave out the nonce. `scripts/record-sovereign-discovery.mjs` records a core's discovery document, and `sovereign-discovery.test.ts` ties the fake to `fixtures/sovereign-discovery.json` (what sign-in relies on in it: the endpoints, EdDSA, PKCE S256, basic auth, the scopes and claims; and that the fake agrees). Tests: 21 over real requests through `auth.handler` against the fake (a new person signed in as the account Sovereign says; the request core is sent; the same person again by `sub`, and when their address changed at core; a missing name; tokens stored encrypted; an ID token with the wrong key, for another client or without the nonce refused with no account left behind; a declined consent; a callback used twice; sign-out staying local; no password routes and no two-factor or passkey routes on a Sovereign-only instance; the first person in and the second refused by the server; a local account's address refused; a different address making its own account; core unreachable leaving the instance up, a Sovereign start refused, and a password still working; discovery without keys refused; no Sovereign start when only local is on), 3 of the settings, and 12 browser tests in Chromium and WebKit on two instances of their own against a fake core run as a process (`e2e/fake-sovereign-server.ts`): Sovereign alone (nothing local to find, the first person in and signing out staying local, a second turned back with the reason, a declined consent), and both ways (each signs in; a Sovereign login with a local account's address refused with what to do). **Seen to fail with it broken:** implicit linking switched on (a Sovereign login attached to the local account), and `requireIdTokenVerification` off (an unverifiable provider trusted). **Left out, and said so:** the recording is Fjord's, of a real core as it was then (an issuer with the path `/api/auth`), and has not been re-recorded here (it needs the network and a core: `node scripts/record-sovereign-discovery.mjs <issuer>`); the contract now says the issuer has no path, which the settings accept either way; **Settings > Security still shows a password to an account that has none** (a Sovereign-only account), which is 2.6's; the sqld and PostgreSQL legs were not run when this was written; the unit tests of sign-in have since passed on both (task 2.9), and the browser tests have been run on the file leg only.

#### ✅ 2.6 — Linking a Sovereign identity

**Goal:** a local account can add Sovereign, under the rules that keep one person's identity from reaching another's account.

**Design:** §6. **Depends on:** 2.5, 2.7.

**Technical:** only from a signed-in session; only when Sovereign's email is the account's own, case aside; an identity already linked cannot link
again; unlinking is a fresh-session action and never removes the last way in. Afterwards, Sovereign sign-in finds the account by `sub`, whatever its
address later becomes. Fjord's `web-1a.md` 1a.4 is the list of tests. In Settings > Security.

**Accepts when:** each rule has a test seen to fail with it switched off, on all three databases.

**Done as:** better-auth's account linking, configured in `createAuth` (task 2.5) and given its screens: **Settings > Security > Sovereign** (`SovereignPanel`, on an instance that has a core) shows the address the account must share and a "Link Sovereign" button, which sends the person to Sovereign and back to `/account?section=security&linked=1`, or with the reason in `?error=` (`email_does_not_match`, `account_already_linked_to_different_user`, and the others `oauthErrorMessage` knows). **The rules, each with its test, seen to fail with it switched off:** linking is only from a signed-in session (a start with no session is a 401 with no address to go to); **only when Sovereign's address is the account's own, case aside** (`allowDifferentEmails: false`), and a token that does not say the address is verified still links, since core's contract is that it is; an identity that is already linked to another account cannot link again; **unlinking is a fresh-session action** (`FRESH_SESSION_SECONDS`, ten minutes, in place of better-auth's day: a session made an hour ago gets `SESSION_NOT_FRESH` and the page says to sign in again) and **never removes the last way in** (`allowUnlinkingAll: false`: a Sovereign-only account's one row stays, and so does anyone else's); after linking, a Sovereign sign-in finds the account **by `sub`**, whatever Sovereign's address becomes, and it is the local account, with its own name and address; unlinking puts the address back to being refused (`account_not_linked`); a person with an authenticator app is not asked for its code when signing in through Sovereign, and the panel says so. **What the places left for 2.6 now do (2.7, 2.8):** an account that came in through Sovereign alone has no password, authenticator app or passkeys to manage, and Security says so; the routes behind those refuse it too (`NO_PASSWORD_ACCOUNT`, a 403 from a hook, for `two-factor/enable`, `get-totp-uri`, `generate-backup-codes` and the passkey registration routes, so that the page is not the only thing that says so), and an account with a password is not touched by that rule; and **deleting the account asks such an account for a session made in the last ten minutes instead of a password** (`SESSION_NOT_FRESH` otherwise; the preview says which it asks for, and a password it does not have is no way round it). Tests: 14 more in `sovereign.test.ts` over real requests (the six rules above and their neighbours: the address matched without regard to case, a claim of an unverified address, another person's account not unlinked, the two-factor case, the stale session, the routes refused to a passwordless account and not to the other kind), 1 in `delete-account.test.ts`, and 10 more browser tests in Chromium and WebKit (a local account links and signs in as itself; a different address is refused with the reason; unlinking, and the login turned back; a Sovereign-only account's Security page with no password to manage and nothing to unlink; deleting such an account with no password and the instance open to its first person again). **Seen to fail with it broken:** `allowDifferentEmails`, `allowUnlinkingAll`, the fresh-session age, and the hook for passwordless accounts, each failing exactly its own test. **Also fixed:** the browser tests' extra instances (keys, security, and these) were each given a database of their own and shared one sign-in store, `tusk_auth.db` beside it, so they shared accounts and a session cookie (a port does not separate cookies); each now has a directory of its own, which is what 2.4's "its own first account" said and was not. **Left out, and said so:** the sqld and PostgreSQL legs were not run when this was written and have since passed (task 2.9), the browser tests on the file leg only; the full browser suite once more; and a person's way back from a stale session is to sign out and in again, not a button that asks Sovereign for a fresh login (`prompt=login`), which core's contract does not promise.

#### ✅ 2.7 — Settings

**Goal:** one dialog, from the avatar menu, with Profile, Security, Preferences, Notifications, Data and Activity, each filled as far as what exists.

**Design:** §19. **Depends on:** 7.8.

**Technical:** `/account?section=`: a `Dialog` over the page on a desktop and a full page on a phone, through a parallel route, as Fjord's
`app/@modal/(.)account`, with a section list beside the content. **Profile:** name, email, avatar (what workspaces see). **Security:** password (asks
for the old one, ends other sessions), signed-in devices with sign-out elsewhere, and the places 2.4 and 2.6 fill. An account that came through
Sovereign has no password, two-factor or passkey parts, and says so. **Preferences:** theme, first day of the week, the sort of My workspaces (16.4).
**Notifications:** filled by 8.1. **Data:** last synced, and the places 9.1, 10.3, 14.2, 17.5 and 2.8 fill. **Activity:** filled by 17.6 (the person's own activity, on the boards they can still see), and the
devices' last sign-in. `UserMenu` opens it: a header of name and email, Settings, Sign out, and a way back to the instance when a core is set. Each
section says when it needs a network. `account_settings` gains `first_day_of_week` and `workspace_sort`, saved through `PATCH /api/settings`.

**Accepts when:** a local and a Sovereign-only account each see the right parts, and the routes behind them refuse the other kind; the dialog opens over
the Desk and over a board and closes back to them; an address with a section opens it there; a preference saved here holds on a second device.

**Done as:** Settings is at `/account?section=` in `src/account/`, and from the avatar menu's _Settings_ it is a `Dialog` over the page it was opened from, by an intercepting route (`app/(app)/@modal/(.)account`, with the `modal` slot in the app's layout and a `default.tsx` that is empty) and a page of its own when the address is typed or reloaded; closing the dialog goes back (`router.back()`). The menu item is a soft navigation (`router.push`), since the design system's `href` item is a plain link that would load the page and not intercept it. `query.ts` is the pure rule for which section an address names (only four exist, and anything else, including what an object inherits, is Profile). The shell shows one section and keeps the others mounted, so a half-typed password is not lost by looking at another. **Profile:** the initials, the name (saved through `authClient.updateUser`, and the menu follows through `router.refresh()`), and the address, shown and not changed. **Security:** the password, asking for the old one and ending every other session (`changePassword` with `revokeOtherSessions`), the new one held to the instance's minimum and to being typed twice; and the devices this account is signed in on, each of which can be signed out, with _Sign out everywhere else_. A device is named from its browser's words (`deviceName`, tested), never the raw string. **Preferences:** the theme, applied at once and kept on the device (`tusk.theme`). **Data:** when this device last heard from the server. Anything that needs a connection says so when there is none. The access table has `/account` and the route scanner reads `(.)account` as that address. Tests: the section rule, `deviceName`, a change of password over real requests (a wrong old one changes nothing, the right one ends the other session, keeps this one, the old password stops working), and 9 browser tests in Chromium and WebKit (the dialog over the Desk and back, an address with a section, the sections keeping what was typed, a name changed and put back, a wrong password, a short or mismatched one, the devices, the theme, Data). **Not as the epic said:** there are no Sovereign-only accounts to tell apart yet (2.5 and 2.6), so every account sees the password; the avatar is the initials, since nothing here uploads a picture; the first day of the week and the workspaces' sort are not here, since nothing reads them (4.2, 16.4); and Notifications, Activity, and what fills Data (export, backup, encryption, deleting the account) join with 8.1, 17.6, 9.1, 10.3, 14.2 and 2.8.

#### ✅ 2.8 — People, and deleting an account

**Goal:** one routine deletes everything of an account, hands on what is shared, and a person can run it.

**Design:** §22. **Depends on:** 2.7, 16.3, 18.6.

**Technical:**

- `src/sovereign/people.ts`: `People.deletePerson(accountId)`. **The Desk:** every list, card, item and series (soft-deleted included), list keys,
  tombstones and applied mutations. **Workspaces:** for each where they are the only owner, the longest-standing other member becomes owner (raising a
  `workspace_passed` notice), and one with nobody else is deleted with its boards; then the person is removed from every workspace and board and
  unassigned, as 16.3's removal does. Their comments, cards and activity stay, resolving to "Deleted account". **The rest:** notices for and about them,
  settings, the backup connection and credential, rate counters, then passkeys, two-factor, sessions, sign-ins, and the account. The row linking a `sub`
  goes last, so a run that stopped can find the account. Each store's part in transactions; the two stores cannot share one, so it is written to be run
  again.
- Settings > Data → Delete account: asks for the password, or a fresh session for a Sovereign-only account; says what goes, which workspaces pass to
  whom, and that a backup repository does not. This device's local database goes too; other devices learn at their next sync.
- `people.test.ts` seeds **one row of every table** for the person and for a second person sharing a workspace and a board with them, by asking the
  schema for the tables, so a table left out fails it.

**Accepts when:** after deletion nothing of that account is on any table; a workspace they solely owned now has the other person as owner, with their
boards, cards and the deleted person's comments intact; a workspace with nobody else is gone; running it again does nothing and does not fail; a new
table with an account column fails the test until it is handled.

**Done as:** **`src/sovereign/people.ts`**: `deletePerson(deps, accountId)`, one routine in a fixed order and **safe to run again** (a test runs it twice, and a stopped run is finished by running it again; the two stores cannot share a transaction): **(1)** the shared work (`leaveAllWorkspaces` in `src/workspaces/person.ts`, since only `src/workspaces/` touches the workspace tables): a workspace they are the only owner of passes to **the member who has been in it longest** (by when they joined, then id), who is told by a new notice kind, `workspace_passed`, and one with nobody else in it is deleted with its boards by the same `deleteWorkspace` the delete operation uses; each workspace is one transaction; they are taken out of every workspace and board and unassigned, as removing a person does; **(2)** the Desk: every list, card, item and series (deleted ones too), the tombstones and the record of applied mutations; **(3)** the backup connection and the token sealed for it, the rate counters (keyed `kind:account`), the account's settings, and every notice for them or about them; **(4)** the sign-in store: sessions, sign-ins, pending verifications named by the address, **and the account last**, so that a run that stopped can find it (the passkeys, second factors and Sovereign identity of 2.3 to 2.6 are to be added to this list before the account row, which the test will insist on). **Their comments, the cards they made, the activity they caused and a publication stay** on boards that carry on, with an id that no longer answers to a name, so they are shown as "Deleted account"; nothing is copied to keep the name. **The backup repository is not touched.** **`/api/account/delete`** (`src/sovereign/delete-account.ts`, in `routes.ts`): `GET` is the preview (which workspaces pass to whom or are deleted, and the repository that is connected); `POST { password }` asks for **the password** (checked by the sign-in library, so a stolen session cannot delete an account), counted at five tries in ten minutes, and runs the routine for the session's account and no other: 204, 403 for a password that is not theirs (nothing deleted), 400, 429. A Sovereign-only account has no password: it is asked for a session made in the last ten minutes instead (task 2.6). **Settings > Data > Delete account** says what goes before it asks, then the password; on success this device's copy, its identity and its key are removed and the page starts from the front; **other devices find out at their next sync, when their session has ended**. Tests: **`people.test.ts` asks the schema for its tables and fails for one that is in none of three lists (goes with the person, stays as shared work, not about a person)**, then seeds a row of the person's in each table, and for a second person who shares a workspace and a board with them (a workspace the first owns alone and shares, one they own alone, one the second owns with the first in it, cards, comments, labels, assignments, a checklist item, a publication, a Desk with a purged deletion, a token, a connection, counters, a verification), deletes the first, twice, and finds none of it where it goes, the workspace passed and the person told, the one with nobody else gone with its boards, the shared work and its comment, activity and publication still there, and **the second person's Desk, token, counters, account and sessions whole**; the choice of who a workspace passes to (earliest member, told alone); and the endpoint with two real accounts (who may call it and from where, the preview, a wrong or absent password deleting nothing, the rate, the right one deleting that account only and ending its session); and a browser test (the wrong password is refused, the right one deletes, and the address no longer signs in). **Each rule was seen to fail with it broken:** the credentials, the tombstones, the counters, the deletion of a workspace with nobody else, the passing on, the notices about them and the choice of who. **Not run:** the same tests on sqld and PostgreSQL (Docker was not running then; the unit suite has since passed on both, task 2.9); the preview and deletion with a second owner and a board in each of many workspaces at scale.

#### ✅ 2.9 — Notices from core

**Goal:** a core can end a person's sessions and delete their account here.

**Design:** §22. **Depends on:** 2.5, 2.8.

**Technical:** `@sovereignfs/sdk`'s `verifyLogoutToken` and `verifyAccountDeletedToken`, imported in `src/sovereign/verifiers.ts` alone.
`POST /api/sovereign/backchannel-logout` and `/api/sovereign/account-deleted`, rules in `src/sovereign/http.ts`: a bounded form (16 KiB), one rate limit
for both, `400` to refuse, `503` when core could not be asked, `500` for a failure of ours, `200` for a deletion only once done. A replayed `jti`
refused through `rate_bucket`. 404 where Sovereign is not set up. Logs the kind of error, never the token or the person.

**Accepts when:** tests over `createFakeCore`'s real tokens: a good notice works, a replay, a wrong audience and an expired one do not; a browser test
with the fake Sovereign signs someone out from the core's side.

**Done as:** adapted from Fjord Web's. **`src/sovereign/verifiers.ts`** is the one file that imports `@sovereignfs/sdk`'s `verifyLogoutToken` and `verifyAccountDeletedToken` (type, signature against core's keys, issuer, audience, age, event, `jti`, no `nonce`: the SDK holds the rules), and `seenBy` turns a `jti` into "seen before" by counting it against a limit of one in `rate_bucket`, for as long as the notice could be replayed, so another process sees it. **`src/sovereign/http.ts`** holds the HTTP rules of **`POST /api/sovereign/backchannel-logout`** and **`/api/sovereign/account-deleted`**, tested with stand-ins: one rate limit for both (300 a minute across all callers, `429`), a bounded form (16 KiB, read no further than that, and only `application/x-www-form-urlencoded`), `400 {"error":"invalid_request"}` for a token that is wrong in any way, absent or not in a form, `503` when core could not be asked, `500` for a failure of ours, and `200` for a deletion only once it is done (core sends it again until it is); it logs the kind of error and never the token, the person or a message that may name a host. **`identity.ts`** finds the account by the Sovereign `sub` (the `account_id` of the sign-in row whose provider is Sovereign) and ends every session of it; **`server.ts`** makes what the routes are handed, once per process, and is undefined where no core is set, when the routes answer **404**. A logout notice ends every session of the person, wherever they are signed in (a notice that names only core's session is accepted and ends nothing, as Tusk does not know core's session ids); an account-deleted notice runs `People.deletePerson` for the account (2.8), and for a person nobody here knows (never signed in, or already deleted: a retry) answers `200`. The two routes are in `src/access/routes.ts` as public with their reason (a token signed by Sovereign), a `GET` is a `405`. Tests: the SDK's own fake core signs the notices (`createFakeCore` from `@sovereignfs/sdk/testing`), and the verifiers, the handlers and `seenBy` are tried against it (the person each notice names; each of ten ways a token can be wrong, refused with `400` and nothing acted on; one notice not taken for the other; a notice refused the second time and a new one for the same person taken; `503` when core is down, remembering nothing; `500` then `200` for a retried deletion; `seenBy` in the database and forgetting after the window), and the fake Sovereign of 2.5 signs them too, issuer path and all; the sessions of an account are ended and no one else's, twice. The access list's test holds both routes to a reason. Browser tests, in Chromium and WebKit, on the Sovereign-only instance with the fake signing a notice (`e2e/fake-sovereign-server.ts` gained `POST /notice`): signing out at Sovereign signs the person out here on two browsers at once, and they can sign in again; a notice wrong in each way, of the other kind, empty or not a form is `400` and they stay signed in; a replay is `400`; a `GET` is `405`; and a deletion notice deletes the account, a repeat is `200`, and the next person to come is let in as the first. **Left out, and said so:** `createFakeCore` is the SDK's and has no notion of Tusk's own sign-in, so the browser tests use this app's fake; and a retried deletion that failed half way is finished by running `deletePerson` again (it is safe to), which the tests of 2.8 hold, not by anything here.

**Also, on the databases:** the whole unit suite was run for the first time on **sqld 0.24.33 and PostgreSQL 16** (throwaway containers, `TUSK_TEST_*`), and passed after five fixes of things that only those legs could show: four tests of 2.8, 2.1x and 9.x (`backup/endpoint`, `encryption/endpoint`, `sovereign/people`, `sovereign/delete-account`) opened the app's store with `makeStore()` after `twoAccounts()`, and `makeStore()` empties both stores on a shared database, taking the sessions away (they now use `openStoreBesideAuth()`, as `sync/endpoint` always did); two of them wrote their raw SQL for SQLite alone (`"user"`, `verification`, a boolean `0`); and `markSeen` of the Inbox (`ON CONFLICT … DO UPDATE SET inbox_seen_at = … inbox_seen_at …`) was **ambiguous on PostgreSQL**, a real bug that failed the Inbox there (the target is now named `s`). All 1705 tests pass on both, and the file leg. The Playwright suite has been run on the file leg only; on sqld and PostgreSQL it has not.

#### ✅ 2.10 — Hosting on an instance
