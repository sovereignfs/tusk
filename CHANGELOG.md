# Changelog

All notable changes to Tusk are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

Each release's section is also its GitHub release notes: the release workflow reads this file when a release is cut. Write the notes under
`## [Unreleased]` as you go; a release turns them into the version's section.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The repository's conventions: licence, contributing guide, pull request template and dependency updates.
- Formatting with Prettier, linting with ESLint, and tests with Vitest.
- Continuous integration, which runs only what a change can affect.
- Settings are read in one place and checked at start-up: the server stops, naming the setting and never its value, on a wrong address, a partial set of related settings, or an SMTP host with no sender. `.env.example` lists every setting.
- The database, in three forms: an sqld service (the default), a SQLite file and PostgreSQL, chosen by `TUSK_DB_DIALECT`. The server provisions and migrates it when it starts, waits for one that is still starting, and does not start if it cannot. `/api/health` answers only when the database does.
- Migrations generated from a schema per dialect with `pnpm db:generate`, and checked in CI.
- The frame of every page: a header with the app's mark and name, in light and dark and at a phone's width, with pages for a missing or failed route. Typeface and colours come from the design system, and a test refuses a literal colour, an undefined token or a typeface of the app's own.
- Every page is sent with a Content-Security-Policy that has a nonce of its own, with no inline script, handler or `eval` allowed and nothing third-party; every response has the hardening headers, and HSTS over https.
- A runner for background work inside the web process, safe when several processes share a database, switched off in all but one with `TUSK_WORKER=off`, and stopped on SIGTERM.
- Browser tests with Playwright, in Chromium and WebKit, on each database form.
- One image on Debian, not root, with the three ways to run it (an sqld service, a file, PostgreSQL) and a README, and a check that brings each up from nothing, restarts it, and stops it on SIGTERM. Published images carry provenance.
- Local sign-in: the first account is made with an email and password and signs the person in, and later sign-ups are refused by the server unless `TUSK_AUTH_SIGNUP=open`. Sign-in is limited per address, a request from another site is refused, and a wrong password reads the same as an address with no account. With `TUSK_AUTH_LOCAL` off, `/sign-in`, `/sign-up` and `/api/auth` answer 404. The header has a menu with Sign out.
- The sync endpoint, `POST /api/sync`, which a device sends its changes to and pulls what changed from: a change is applied once however often it is sent, only to the signed-in person's own rows, in an order every device agrees on. Nothing in the app uses it yet.
- Signing out removes that person's Desk from the device, after sending what it can and asking before it throws away what it cannot; it needs a connection, so that no one else can use the session. A session that ends elsewhere shows sign-in and no data at the next sync, and keeps what was changed on the device for when the same person signs back in.
- The app for someone signed in: a header, a sidebar of lists with their colour and count of open cards, and a right-hand column that opens for a card and closes without adding a step to go back through. It reads from the device, so it shows at once with or without a network. On a phone the sidebar is left out until the mobile layout.
- Lists: the first visit makes _Backlog_, a + in the sidebar adds a list (also with no network, and it is sent when the connection comes back), and lists are dragged into order, which changes one row. A list that is not there says so and leads back.
- The front address is a page that says what Tusk is, with a way to sign in, for someone who is not signed in, and their first list for someone who is.
- Cards: add one by typing and pressing Enter (it goes to the top and the cursor stays), tick it off, open it in the right-hand column to change its title and description (saved as you leave the field), and delete it with ten seconds to undo. All of it works with no network and is sent when the connection comes back.
- Checklists: items in a card's detail, each with a tick and a title, with a ring on the card's row showing how far through it is. Ticking a card ticks its open items, and leaves the time of those already done; ticking the last item does not tick the card.
- Cards and checklist items are dragged into order, which changes one row, and by keyboard: Space lifts a row, the arrows move it, Space puts it down.
- Deleting a card takes its checklist with it, and Undo brings back exactly those, not one that was deleted before.
- Deleted cards, items and lists are removed for good after seven days, and a device that was away learns of it the next time it connects; one that was away for more than ninety days starts again from what the server holds, after sending what it had changed.
- A list can be sorted by the order you made, date created, due date or title, and the choice is remembered for that list on every device; under any sort but your own order a card cannot be dragged. The Completed section opens and closes as you left it, can be hidden with _Active_, and has _Delete completed_, with Undo.
- A card can be moved to another list from its detail (its checklist stays with it), and starred with a star on its row or in the detail.
- Sign-in through a Sovereign core: set `TUSK_CORE_URL`, `TUSK_CORE_CLIENT_ID` and `TUSK_CORE_CLIENT_SECRET` and the sign-in page offers "Sign in with Sovereign", alone or beside a password. A person is the account their Sovereign identity says; a Sovereign login whose address is a local account's is refused until the two are linked from a signed-in session, and the server starts, and local sign-in works, when the core is down.
- A local account can link Sovereign from Settings > Security, when the Sovereign account uses the same email address, and can unlink it (after a recent sign-in) as long as it has another way in. An account that came in through Sovereign alone has no password, authenticator app or passkeys to manage, and deletes itself on a recent sign-in instead of a password.
- Sovereign can sign a person out of Tusk everywhere, and delete their Tusk account when they delete theirs, by signed notices Tusk checks against its keys (`/api/sovereign/backchannel-logout`, `/api/sovereign/account-deleted`). `sovereign.manifest.json` is now complete, so a Sovereign instance can host Tusk (`sv app add`): see `deploy/README.md`.
- Where whoever runs Tusk has registered a GitHub App, Settings > Data > Backup offers _Connect with GitHub_: the person installs the App on one private repository and Tusk keeps no token, asking GitHub for a short-lived one each time. Removing the App or the repository on GitHub disconnects it. Connecting with a token still works.
- On a phone, the sidebar and the Desk's lists are in a drawer the header's button opens, a card opens as a page of its own, a board's lists scroll sideways and stop at each, and the card's dialog fills the screen. The addresses and the data are the same at every width.
- Accessibility: every page is checked with axe in light and dark, on a desktop and a phone, in both browsers; a drag of a list, a card or a checklist item by the keyboard says what it picked up and where it is (it used to read out an id), and the header's name, a list's handle and the due-date and repeat buttons are named by what is drawn on them.

### Changed

- Workspaces: made from the + in the sidebar, listed under _My workspaces_ (and _Shared with me_, for those a person is in), renamed by an owner, and deleted by typing the name, which takes every board and card in it. A workspace that is not yours is the page for what is not here.
- The server has the tables of workspaces, boards and cards, and one door for writing to them, `POST /api/ops`: an operation with its permission in the statement that does the work, answered the same whether the thing is not there or is not yours. Nothing in the app uses it yet.
- A workspace's people: owners add a person by the exact email of an account that exists here, make others owners, and remove them; anyone can leave; a workspace always keeps an owner. Removing or leaving takes the person off the workspace's boards and unassigns them, and their comments stay. Adding by address is limited to 30 an hour for each account. All of it is in the workspace's settings, from the gear on its page.
- Boards: a workspace shows its boards as tiles and has a tile to create one, with a name and any colour. A board is made with a _Backlog_ list; its members add lists, rename them, drag them by their header or move them from the menu, and delete them after being told how many cards go with them. A change shows at once and is undone if it is refused. The board reads again when it was changed from elsewhere, within 20 seconds or when you come back to it, and says it needs a connection when offline.
- The boards page: the Desk as a tile with its open and due-today counts (there offline), then each workspace with its people, a gear for its settings, its boards and a tile to create one; archived boards are behind _Archived_. The order of your own workspaces (name, date created, recently updated) is saved with your account and is the same on every device. The sidebar's entry is _Boards_.
- Cards on a board: add a card at the top of a list (from the column or the list's menu), drag it by its whole tile within a list or to another, move it with the keyboard (Space, arrows, Space), archive it from its menu, and restore it to its place or delete it for good from _Archived cards_.
- The card dialog: a click or Enter on a card opens it over its board, at its own address, so it can be linked to and reloaded. The title and the description save as you leave them and the card behind follows at once; a title emptied goes back to the one it had; the card can be archived from it. Closing it replaces the address.
- A board's menu: Settings (name, description and any colour), Archive and Restore, and Delete, which asks for the board's name. An archived board is read-only for everyone, its owners included, until it is restored. They are for a board's owners and the workspace's owners.
- Sharing a board: the board's people are avatars in its header, which open a Share dialog. A board's owners and the workspace's owners set who can see it (everyone in the workspace, or only its people and the workspace's owners), add people from the workspace, give roles and remove people; anyone on it can leave. A viewer of a public board joins it with one click and can then change it.
- One idea of "today", the same on the Desk and on boards and in any time zone, and your account keeps the time zone your browser reports (updated on the next load if you move).
- Due dates and times, on the Desk and on boards, from one picker: Today, Tomorrow, Next week, a calendar, and a time once there is a date. They show as Today, Tomorrow or the day, in your own time form, and in red when overdue. A time cannot exist without a date.
- Labels: each board has its own, of any colour, with a name or without. Put them on a card from its dialog (a tick for each, and make, edit or delete from there or from the board's settings); a card's tile shows them as small bars of colour, and the dialog as chips with the name in black or white. Deleting a label takes it off every card.
- Assignees: a card is assigned from its dialog to members of its board, shown as avatars on its tile. Only a member of the board can be assigned, and being assigned by someone else leaves a notice for the Inbox (none when you assign yourself).
- A checklist on every card, in its dialog: add items (the cursor stays for the next), tick them, edit them in place, drag them into order or move them with the keyboard, and delete them. A progress bar shows in the dialog and "2/5" on the card's tile, which follows at once.
- Comments on cards: written in the card's dialog, newest first and a page at a time, shown as text with their line breaks. Only the author edits a comment, and the author or a board's owner deletes it. The card's tile shows how many there are. The card's assignees and its maker are sent a notice (never the author).
- The Inbox: notices for being assigned a card, a comment on a card you made or are assigned to, and being added to a workspace or a board, grouped by day, each opening what it is about; and the cards assigned to you across your boards by due date. The sidebar's Inbox entry counts what you have not seen, on every device. Settings > Notifications turns each kind on or off. Notices are kept 90 days, at most 500.
- Activity: what happened on a board, by whom and when, in plain sentences ("Ana moved Fix login from Backlog to Doing"): a dialog from the board's menu, a tab in each card, and your own in Settings > Activity. It reads the same after a card is renamed, and stays after a card or list is deleted.
- Moving and cloning cards: a card is moved from its dialog to another list, or to a list of another board of the workspace that you are a member of, taking its comments, checklist and history with it; its labels are matched there by name and colour, and the dialog says who is taken off it. A card can be cloned from its dialog or the tile's menu: a copy below it, with the checklist unticked, and no comments.
- Search on a board: a field in its header filters the cards as you type, by title, label and assignee; case and accents do not matter ("é" finds "É"), a line says how many cards are hidden, `/` goes to the field and Esc clears it. Nothing can be dragged while a search is on.
- Publishing a board: its owners give it a public, read-only address from the Share dialog. Anyone can open it without signing in and sees the board, its cards, labels, due dates and checklists, and none of the people, comments or activity. Unpublishing takes the address away for good; publishing again makes a different one. Your published boards are listed in Settings > Data.
- Overdue on the Desk: a card is overdue from its time, in your own time zone (a date with no time, from tomorrow), and under Custom order what is overdue or due today floats to the top without changing anyone's order. A new Overdue choice beside All and Active shows only what is overdue.
- Starred on the Desk: a list's Show control gains Starred, which shows only its open starred cards. The choice (All, Active, Starred, Overdue) is now kept with the list and on your other devices. Taking the star off a card while Starred is on removes it from view without moving it.
- Search on the Desk: a field at the top of the lists column finds cards by title, description and checklist item as you type, ignoring case and accents, with every word needing to be found. The results show where each card is and the checklist items that matched, newest change first. It runs on your device, so it works offline, and what you type is never treated as a pattern.
- Choosing several cards on the Desk: hold ctrl (or cmd on a Mac) and click a card to choose it, then click more; a bar offers Complete, Move to list, Delete and Cancel for all of them at once. A repeating card completed this way makes its next, and one Undo brings back everything a delete took.
- Keyboard shortcuts on the Desk and on boards: n adds a card, / searches, j and k (or the arrow keys, once a card is focused) move between cards, e completes the focused card on the Desk, Enter opens it and Esc closes. ? lists them. They do nothing while you type in a field, or with ctrl, cmd or alt held.
- Tusk can be installed as an app, on a computer or a phone (Settings says how on an iPhone), and then your Desk opens with no connection, from what is on your device. Boards, the Inbox and Settings say they need a connection. The app keeps only its own files and one page that holds nothing of you; your cards stay on your device and are cleared when you sign out.
- Working offline is documented (`docs/working-offline.md`): what your Desk does with no connection, what needs one, and where it stops. Opening and closing a card no longer asks the server for anything, so it is quicker online and works offline. A board you have open goes read-only the moment your connection drops, and says why.
- Export: Settings > Data saves your Desk, or everything (your Desk and the workspaces you own), as one JSON file; a board's menu exports that board, and a workspace's owners can export the workspace. Your Desk exports even with no connection. People appear by name and address, never by id, and the file holds no link, key or password.
- Import: Settings > Data reads a file made by Export, says what is in it ("your Desk: 3 lists, 40 cards; 1 workspace, 4 boards") and asks before adding it. It always adds, so importing twice makes two copies. Your Desk imports even with no connection; a board goes into a workspace you choose, and people who are not in that workspace are not assigned. A file that is not right is refused whole, saying where, and nothing is changed.
- Import also reads the file the Sovereign Tasks plugin saves: its lists become Desk lists and its tasks cards, subtasks become checklist items, notes the description, favourites stars, and repeats are kept where their rule can be read. Before anything is written it lists what it changed or could not carry (saved views, assignments, a repeat it could not read, titles cut to the limit).
- For operators: `TUSK_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (32 random bytes, in base64 or hex) is now checked when the server starts, and is what will seal a repository's token for backup. To change it, set the new key and put the old one in `TUSK_ENCRYPTION_KEY_PREVIOUS`; the server moves what is kept to the new key when it starts. It stops, naming the key, if something kept needs a key it was not given.
- For operators: `TUSK_GITHUB_API_URL` names GitHub's API for a GitHub Enterprise Server (default `https://api.github.com`). It must be https, or http on localhost, and the server will not start with one that is not.
- Backup to git, connecting: Settings > Data can connect one private GitHub repository with a fine-grained token (address as `owner/name` or its GitHub address). It is checked before anything is kept: the token is accepted, can see the repository and write to it, the repository is private and has something in it, and each refusal says what to do. A token that can reach other repositories is accepted with a warning. The token is kept sealed under the server's key and shown nowhere; Disconnect deletes it and leaves the repository alone.
- Backup to git, Back up now: with a repository connected, Settings > Data has **Back up now**, which writes your Desk and the workspaces you own as one JSON file to `tusk/<date>_<time>.json` (UTC) in the repository, as a new commit on its default branch with nothing else in the repository touched, and links to it. The repository is checked again each time and nothing is written if it has become public. At most once a minute.
- Backup to git, automatic: Settings > Data can make backups automatically, every hour, 6 hours, day (the default) or week. An automatic backup only writes a new file when something has changed since the last one (your Desk, or a workspace you own), and Settings shows when it last checked and when it will next. Back up now always writes.
- A backup that fails says so: Settings > Data shows why, in words and with what to do. A failure that will mend itself (GitHub unreachable, asking to be left alone) is tried again at the next time. One that will not (a token that no longer works, a repository that is gone, made public or empty) stops the automatic backups, tells you once in the Inbox, and the backups start again when you back up now, connect again or choose automatic again.
- Delete account: Settings > Data can delete your account with your password. It first says what goes: your Desk, your account and the token saved for backups; workspaces you alone own pass to the member who has been in them longest (who is told in their Inbox) or are deleted if nobody else is in them; you are taken out of the rest. Your comments and the cards you made stay on boards that carry on, shown as "Deleted account". Your backup repository is not touched.
- Email, when the server has an SMTP server (`TUSK_SMTP_HOST`, `_FROM`, and a user and password if it needs them): a new address is confirmed by a link before its first sign-in, and the link signs you in; a forgotten password is reset from the sign-in page by a link that can be used once, and using it signs you out everywhere else. Without it, nothing changes: no confirmation, no reset, and those pages and routes are not there.
- Settings > Security has an authenticator app (scan a code or type a key; ten single-use backup codes; it is on only once a code from the app has worked; turning it off or making new codes asks for your password) and passkeys (add, name, rename, remove; one signs you in with no password, on this address). Signing in with the app asks for a code after the password.
- A key for your Desk: the Desk offers, once, to be yours alone. Create a key (you must download it and say you saved it: nobody can recover it), use a key you already have, or Skip. On a device that does not have the key the Desk shows nothing of itself and asks for it, once, until you sign out; boards and the Inbox are unaffected. Settings > Data says where this device stands. The Desk's content is now sealed on a Desk with a key: titles, descriptions, dates, repeats and lists' names and colours are ciphertext on the server, and a card's checklist and repeat move with it to another list. A Desk that already has things in it can be moved to a key of your own from Settings > Data: it is sealed in the background while you use it, carries on if you close the page, and finishes only when nothing is left in the clear; changes made on a device that had been away are sealed, not lost. For operators: `TUSK_DB_ENCRYPTION=on` (the operator's own encryption) is not built yet, so the server refuses to start with it rather than say it encrypts when it does not.
- Backups and exports of a Desk with a key of its own: the backup file holds your Desk as ciphertext with its wrapped keys and says so, restoring it needs your key (import says when it is missing, or is another), and the Desk is imported again under new ids and sealed again. Backups wait while the Desk is being sealed, and then until you confirm that the files already in your repository stay plain. Exporting says that the file you save is not sealed.
- Repeating cards on the Desk: a card can repeat daily, on weekdays, weekly, monthly, yearly or by a custom rule (every N days, weeks, months or years, on chosen weekdays, ending never, on a date or after N times), set from its detail. Ticking it makes the next, due after today, with its checklist unticked; two devices that tick it make one. Does not repeat stops it. Changing a repeating card's date moves the repeat with it (a weekly card moved to a Tuesday repeats on Tuesdays), and clearing the date ends it; renaming or editing it applies to the occurrences still to come, never to the ones you have done. Unticking a card you ticked by mistake takes away the next one, as it was. If you had changed the next one it stays, and the card you reopened becomes a one-off. Moving a repeating card to another list moves the repeat, and deleting one can be undone with it still repeating.

- The app is called **Tusk**, not Tasks. Every setting is `TUSK_…` (it was `TASKS_…`), the databases are `tusk` and `tusk_auth` (files `tusk.db` and `tusk_auth.db`), the session cookie is `tusk.session_token`, the database kept in the browser is `tusk-desk-<account>`, and the image is `ghcr.io/sovereignfs/tusk`. Nothing was released under the old names, so there is no alias: set the new ones, and a database made under the old names starts again.
- The Desk's data is four tables of its own, `desk_list`, `desk_card`, `desk_check` and `desk_series`: a card has a description (what were notes), and a checklist item is a row of its own and no longer a task with a parent. The sync protocol is version 2, so a device that speaks version 1 is told to update, and the database kept in the browser starts again. The database migrations are squashed into one; a development database made before this is made again (`pnpm seed` makes an account worth looking at).
- A card is opened by `?card=` (it was `?task=`). The Desk is at `/desk` and `/desk/<list>` (it was `/home` and `/lists/<id>`).
- A card can be marked In progress as well as Open and Done, from the Status control in its detail; a small turning mark by its title shows it. Done is never lost to a later In progress from a device that had not heard of it.
- Settings, from the avatar menu: a dialog over the page, and a page of its own at `/account`. Profile (the name), Security (the password, which ends every other session, and the devices signed in, each of which can be signed out), Preferences (the theme) and Data (when this device last synced).
- A list can be any colour, picked from the dot beside its name: a suggestion, or a colour of one's own. A card can be dragged from anywhere on its row, and a click on the row's free space opens it.
- The app has a sidebar at the left of every signed-in page, which is full, a rail of icons, or hidden, from a button in the header and one on the sidebar itself, and is remembered on that device for the Desk and for the other pages. The Desk opens it as a rail. On a phone there is none yet.

### Removed

### Fixed

- Opening the Inbox no longer fails on PostgreSQL (a column named twice in one statement was ambiguous there).
